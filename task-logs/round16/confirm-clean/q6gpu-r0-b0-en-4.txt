HTTP caching reduces bandwidth and latency by allowing clients (like browsers) to reuse previously fetched resources instead of downloading them again. It works through a combination of **freshness checks** (using `Cache-Control`) and **validation** (using `ETag` and `If-None-Match`).

### Key Mechanisms

1. **`Cache-Control` (Freshness)**
   - Sent by the server in the response.
   - Tells the client **how long** the resource is considered "fresh" (e.g., `max-age=3600` means valid for 1 hour).
   - If the resource is fresh, the client uses the cached copy **without contacting the server**.

2. **`ETag` (Entity Tag)**
   - A unique identifier (usually a hash) representing the **exact version** of the resource.
   - Sent by the server in the response.
   - Used for **conditional requests** when the cache is stale or needs validation.
