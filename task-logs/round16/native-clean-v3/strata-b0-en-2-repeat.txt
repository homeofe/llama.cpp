Introducing a message queue (MQ) between two services is a classic architectural decision that shifts the system from **synchronous coupling** to **asynchronous decoupling**. Here’s a breakdown of the trade-offs:

### ✅ What It Buys You (Benefits)

1. **Decoupling**
   - **Temporal Decoupling**: The producer doesn’t need to wait for the consumer to be available. If the consumer is down, messages are stored until it recovers.
   - **Logical Decoupling**: Producers don’t need to know the identity, number, or implementation details of consumers. They just publish to a topic/queue.

2. **Resilience & Fault Tolerance**
   - **Buffering**: Spikes in traffic are absorbed by the queue, preventing downstream services from being overwhelmed.
   - **Retry Logic**: Failed messages can be retried automatically, improving reliability without blocking the producer.

3. **Scalability**
   - **Independent Scaling**: Producers and consumers can scale independently. You can add more consumers to handle higher load without changing the producer.
   - **Load Leveling**: Consumers can process messages at their own pace, smoothing out bursts.
